5 Food Plant Flooring Requirements Inspectors Care About

USDA does not tell you what flooring brand to install. They care whether the floor performs.

Home / Blog / Food Plant Flooring Requirements
April 22, 2026Compliance

USDA does not tell you what flooring to install. They tell you what your floor has to do. Most facilities fail on at least one requirement.

We have installed floors in food and beverage plants across the country, and these are the same findings we see on inspection reports over and over. That is why we formulate our own systems and install them with our own crews. One company, start to finish.

1. Cleanable

A food plant floor has to clean up without trapping residue in cracks, open joints, porous patches, or failing coating edges. Seamless resinous flooring helps remove the weak points that sanitation crews fight every day.

2. Durable

Forklifts, washdown, thermal shock, cleaning chemicals, dropped tools, and production traffic punish the floor constantly. SaniCrete STX is built for those conditions with cementitious urethane and stainless steel reinforcement.

3. Properly Sloped

Standing water is a sanitation problem. Floors need to move water toward drains without creating birdbaths around equipment or walls. Slope correction is often just as important as the flooring material itself.

4. Sanitary at Transitions

Inspectors look hard at drains, curbs, wall lines, and penetrations. Those are the places where open seams and sharp corners become harborage points. Integrated cove base and clean drain details matter.

5. Safe Under Wet Conditions

Slip resistance has to be balanced with cleanability. Too smooth creates safety risk. Too aggressive can hold soil and make sanitation harder. The right texture depends on traffic, washdown, footwear, and the room's process.

If a floor was flagged during an inspection, the right fix is not just another coating layer. The right fix starts with why it failed: substrate, slope, drains, wall transitions, traffic, chemistry, or thermal cycling.
